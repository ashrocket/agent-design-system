2. Challenge the alignment criticism

Correction: the entire CTA content group is geometrically centered. The 282.14 × 53px button has equal 21px outer insets including its border. Its 30px gap plus 19px arrow displaces the label's box center 24.5px left of the button center. That is expected for a centered label-plus-icon group; it does not prove a centering defect.

The long label and large internal gap can still look awkward. Reducing the gap to 10–12px and shortening the label is a proposed visual refinement, not an existing rule. If the intended component centers the label independently of a trailing icon, define equal left/right icon slots and test that different anatomy explicitly.

The live homepage CTA is about 271.93px wide, close to the local button's 282.14px. Width alone is not evidence that the local button is too wide. Heading, copy, and CTA share the same left coordinate, 682.90625px. Their differing widths are normal for content-sized elements and not prohibited by the documentation.

The two complete hero groups align within 0.7px at their top and bottom at this viewport. The tile tops sit about 40px below the heading because of the kicker. Top-aligning the tiles and heading is an alternative composition, not a mandatory correction. Digit cards already use place-items:center; negative tracking and compensating padding require visual assessment, not an automatic “uncentered” verdict.

Gaps of 22, 12, and 26px do not match the named spacing tokens. However, the live site's own CTA uses 17px and 34px padding. “All valid spacing must be a multiple of eight” is therefore not an established Retailaer rule. A component-level contract must resolve approved exceptions.

Contrast counterexample: blindly replacing muted text with brand grey #787A7A on dark teal #21494A gives about 2.30:1, below the normal-text requirement. Mint on dark teal gives about 8.73:1. The current opaque CTA color pair gives about 6.99:1. These calculations follow the W3C contrast method; they are not a complete accessibility audit.
